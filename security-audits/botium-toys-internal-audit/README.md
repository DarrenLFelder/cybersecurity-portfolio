# Botium Toys Internal Security Audit

## Project Overview

This project documents an internal security audit I conducted for Botium Toys, a fictional organization used as part of the Google Cybersecurity Certificate program.

The purpose of the audit was to evaluate the organization's existing security controls and compliance practices, identify security gaps, assess risk, and recommend improvements that could strengthen the organization's overall security posture.

## Audit Scope

The audit covered Botium Toys' overall security program, including:

- Employee devices and equipment
- Internal network
- Business systems and software
- Data storage and retention
- E-commerce operations
- Legacy systems
- Physical security controls

## Framework

The assessment applied concepts from the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF)** and evaluated security practices associated with:

- PCI DSS
- GDPR
- SOC
- Administrative controls
- Technical controls
- Physical controls

## Risk Assessment

Botium Toys' initial risk assessment resulted in a risk score of **8/10**, indicating a high level of risk.

Key areas of concern included:

- Excessive employee access to sensitive information
- Lack of least privilege
- Lack of separation of duties
- Unencrypted cardholder information
- No intrusion detection system
- No backups of critical data
- No disaster recovery plan
- Weak password requirements
- No centralized password management system
- Inconsistent legacy-system monitoring

## Audit Methodology

I reviewed the organization's defined audit scope, assets, existing security controls, and risk assessment. I then evaluated whether administrative, technical, and physical controls were currently implemented and assessed security practices related to PCI DSS, GDPR, and SOC.

Each finding was evaluated based on the evidence provided in the risk assessment rather than whether a control simply should exist.

## Key Findings

The audit identified several significant security and compliance gaps. Of particular concern were excessive access to sensitive customer and cardholder information, inadequate access controls, lack of encryption, insufficient detection capabilities, and the absence of backups and disaster recovery planning.

Botium Toys did have several existing controls, including a firewall, antivirus protection, physical locks, CCTV surveillance, fire detection and prevention systems, and controls supporting data integrity and availability.

## Remediation Priorities

Based on my assessment, I would prioritize remediation according to the potential risk to the organization and its customers.

### 1. Protect Cardholder and Sensitive Data

Restrict access to sensitive information, implement least privilege and separation of duties, strengthen password management, and encrypt sensitive payment information.

### 2. Strengthen Identity and Access Management

Establish stronger user access policies so employees only have access to systems and information required for their job responsibilities.

### 3. Improve Detection Capabilities

Implement an intrusion detection system (IDS) to improve the organization's ability to identify suspicious or anomalous network activity.

### 4. Establish Business Continuity and Recovery

Create reliable backups of critical information and establish a documented and tested disaster recovery plan.

### 5. Improve Data Governance and Compliance

Properly inventory and classify information, strengthen protection of customer information, and address remaining PCI DSS and GDPR security gaps.

## Key Takeaway

This project helped me understand that a security audit is not simply about identifying missing security tools. Effective security auditing requires evaluating business risk, determining which assets and data require the greatest protection, identifying control and compliance gaps, and prioritizing remediation based on potential impact to the organization.
